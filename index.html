01 AI · Legal tech 2026

Myanmar Law Guide

A bilingual legal-information assistant that answers with citations, not guesses.

Myanmar’s laws are scattered across scanned PDFs, decades of amendments and mixed Burmese/English text. A generic chatbot will confidently invent a section number — which is worse than no answer at all.

  • OCR pipeline turns scanned law books into searchable text with Tesseract mya+eng.
  • Section-aware ingestion embeds law chunks into PostgreSQL + pgvector.
  • Citation-first RAG refuses low-confidence matches and always shows the source section.
  • Laravel 12
  • Flutter
  • pgvector
  • AWS Bedrock
  • Tesseract

02 Health tech · PWA 2026

BloodLink Myanmar

A privacy-first donor network for a country that coordinates blood on Facebook.

Urgent requests spread through social groups. Donor phone numbers are posted publicly, eligibility is unknown, and receivers waste hours calling people who cannot donate.

  • Compatibility-aware search by blood type, township and live availability.
  • Contact requests keep numbers hidden until the donor accepts.
  • 90/120-day eligibility engine plus weekly windows and daily check-ins.
  • Flutter 3.50
  • Laravel 12
  • Sanctum
  • MySQL 8.4
  • PWA

04 Enterprise 2026

Licence Service Platform

A back office for a licence-service business — from intake to payroll.

Multi-branch licence services involve agents, appointments, vehicle checks, staged applications, commissions, cash advances and payroll. Spreadsheets could not enforce permissions or show where a case actually stood.

  • Owner / leader / subleader / member hierarchy with a granular permission matrix.
  • Staged application pipeline with activity logs, comments and audit trails.
  • Finance, payroll, expenses and signed owner-only backups in one system.

I’m a software engineer at Global Innovation Consulting (GIC), where I build products for Myanmar and Japan across legal tech, healthcare, logistics, education and insurance.

My favourite work sits where software meets messy real-world operations: a dispatcher coordinating trips, a donor finding a compatible match, a student getting a clear answer, a branch team closing a licence case. I care about the unglamorous parts — permissions, audit trails, data privacy, failure states, and the copy that tells someone what to do next.

I started in PHP and Laravel. Today I move between Laravel APIs, Flutter and React clients, and AWS infrastructure. I’m currently going deeper into DevOps with Terraform, IAM and SSM — and writing a beginner-friendly AWS guide in Burmese and English as I learn.

Clarity beats cleverness.

Ship the version people can understand, maintain and hand over. The clever version can wait.
